A (99% Accurate) Guide to Brotato Weapon Cooldown Mechanics

For precise cooldown calculations, we recommend using the Brotato Codex.

0. Overview

CDavg=Tattack interval (fixed)+Cdelay frames (random)\text{CD}_{\text{avg}} = T_{\text{attack interval (fixed)}} + C_{\text{delay frames (random)}}CDavg​=Tattack interval (fixed)​+Cdelay frames (random)​

The cooldown consists of the recoil animation and, for melee weapons only, the swing animation. These are affected by attack speed and range, and are fixed.

The delay framesCCC are affected by the number of weapons and are random.

In the game, 1 second = 60 frames. Below,ttt andfff denote durations expressed in seconds and frames, respectively.

2. Ranged Weapons

A ranged weapon’s cooldown consists only of two recoil animation segments (wind-up + wind-down).

Recoil animation (f)

frecoil=2⋅tween(r)−1f_\text{recoil} = 2 \cdot \text{tween}(r) - 1frecoil​=2⋅tween(r)−1

Attack interval (f)

f=2⋅tween(r)−1+C\boxed{f = 2 \cdot \text{tween}(r) - 1 + C}f=2⋅tween(r)−1+C​

Dividing by 60 gives the actual cooldown time (s).

Tooltip displayed cooldown (s)

ttooltip=2r+w60\boxed{t_\text{tooltip} = 2r + \frac{w}{60}}ttooltip​=2r+60w​​

3. Melee Weapons

A melee weapon’s cooldown consists of three animation segments (wind-up + swing + wind-down).

Range factor (proportional to weapon range. Positive attack speedaaa reduces the range factor, down to a minimum of712\frac{7}{12}127​ at +215 attack speed; negative attack speed does not increase the range factor)

rangeFactor=max_rangeclamp(70(1+a/3),  70,  120)\text{rangeFactor} = \frac{\text{max\_range}}{\text{clamp}\big(70(1+a/3),\;70,\;120\big)}rangeFactor=clamp(70(1+a/3),70,120)max_range​

Heremax_range\text{max\_range}max_range is the weapon’s range, equivalent to the weapon’s base range plus half of the player’s range stat. For example, with a base range of 150 and a player range stat of 100,max_range=150+100/2=200\text{max\_range} = 150 + 100/2 = 200max_range=150+100/2=200.

Wind-up animation (s)

tpre=rt_\text{pre} = rtpre​=r

Swing animation (s) (split into two parts; higher attack speed and lower range factor make the swing shorter)

tatk=max⁡(0.01,  0.2−a10)+rangeFactor×0.15t_\text{atk} = \max\Big(0.01,\; 0.2 - \frac{a}{10}\Big) + \text{rangeFactor} \times 0.15tatk​=max(0.01,0.2−10a​)+rangeFactor×0.15

Wind-down animation (s) (base value 0.2s; positive attack speed shortens it, negative attack speed leaves it unchanged)

tback=0.21+max⁡(0,a)×3t_\text{back} = \frac{0.2}{1 + \max(0,a) \times 3}tback​=1+max(0,a)×30.2​

4. Random Delay Frames

The random delay frames (f) are integers uniformly distributed betweenmax⁡(1,w−Δ)\max(1, w - \Delta)max(1,w−Δ) andw+Δw + \Deltaw+Δ, whereΔ=min⁡(nw5,5n)\Delta = \min\Big(\frac{nw}{5},5n\Big)Δ=min(5nw​,5n) andnnn is the number of weapons, capped at 6.

Taking the expectation of this uniform distribution gives the mean ofCCC, denoted below asgetAvg\text{getAvg}getAvg. The exact algorithm:

triv=v(v+1)2\text{tri}_v = \frac{v(v+1)}{2}triv​=2v(v+1)​

getAvg(min,max)=(⌈min⌉−min)⌈min⌉+tri⌊max⌋−tri⌈min⌉+(max−⌊max⌋)⌈max⌉max−min\text{getAvg}(\text{min}, \text{max}) = \frac{(\lceil\text{min}\rceil - \text{min})\lceil\text{min}\rceil + \text{tri}_{\lfloor\text{max}\rfloor} - \text{tri}_{\lceil\text{min}\rceil} + (\text{max} - \lfloor\text{max}\rfloor)\lceil\text{max}\rceil}{\text{max} - \text{min}}getAvg(min,max)=max−min(⌈min⌉−min)⌈min⌉+tri⌊max⌋​−tri⌈min⌉​+(max−⌊max⌋)⌈max⌉​

Finally, the minimum and maximum delay frames (f) are:

Cmin=⌊max⁡(1,w−Δ)⌋+1\boxed{C_\text{min} = \lfloor\max(1, w - \Delta)\rfloor + 1}Cmin​=⌊max(1,w−Δ)⌋+1​

Cmax=⌈w+Δ⌉\boxed{C_\text{max} = \lceil w + \Delta\rceil}Cmax​=⌈w+Δ⌉​

From the above calculations we can see that, because (i) the time occupied by the animationstween(f)\text{tween}(f)tween(f) is always 1–2 frames more than thef60\frac{f}{60}60f​ used by the tooltip, and (ii) the mean of the random cooldown frames is always slightly higher than thew60\frac{w}{60}60w​ used by the tooltip, the tooltip’s displayed cooldown is an underestimate. Typically a ranged weapon differs by 1–3 frames, a melee weapon by 2–5 frames, and the random frames differ by roughly 0.5–3.5 frames (0.0833–0.5833s, refer to the figure below), though these can vary further with other variables.

The benefit of attack speed is hard to analyze qualitatively, but for the vast majority of weapons the positive attack speed gain shows no obvious diminishing returns (+100% attack speed generally yields a +80% ~ +110% DPS gain, and +200% attack speed generally yields a +140% or greater DPS gain — this does not apply to SMGs or faster ranged weapons, which have clear attack speed breakpoints), while the penalty for negative attack speed is not very severe (at -100% attack speed, DPS change is generally between -50% and -20%).

As for the effect of range on melee weapons, a +200 range stat leads to a DPS change of -4% ~ -14%, while a -200 range stat yields a +5% ~ +20% DPS gain.
